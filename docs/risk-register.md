# Shooting Brake Risk Register

## Purpose and status

This register turns the feasibility conditions and revised stop criteria for the architecture in [`architecture.md`](architecture.md) into explicit decisions. Correctness failures are governed by [`correctness.md`](correctness.md).

Nothing in this register is marked resolved. The source material provides design assessments, required measurements, and decision rules; it does not establish that the current code, host, links, kernels, or service have passed them. In the tables below:

- **Hard stop** means the architecture must be reconsidered when the trigger occurs.
- **Conditional decision gate** means the design may continue only in the narrower operating mode stated by the gate.
- **Feasibility assumption** means a premise that still needs direct evidence. “Strongly feasible” is an upstream assessment, not a verified local result.
- **Invalid assumption** means the premise must not be used for planning under the current design. Work resumes by removing the premise or obtaining the specifically required proof, not by relabeling it as resolved.

Terms such as “bounded,” “materially,” “frequent,” “regularly,” “negligible,” and “too high” deliberately retain the source language. This document does not invent numeric thresholds. A measurement plan must define them before a gate is run.

## Revised hard-stop conditions

These nine conditions supersede looser earlier planning. Unless the item-specific action says otherwise, occurrence requires architecture reconsideration rather than silent degradation.

| ID | Trigger | Evidence needed | Required action | Criterion to resume |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Any B70 PCIe link remains at Gen1 ×1. | Per-card topology audit: negotiated generation and width, root port/switch, and stable transfer measurements under simultaneous five-GPU load. | Reconsider the architecture and correct or avoid the unusable topology; do not project performance from nominal device capability. | Repeat the physical audit and transport measurements after the topology changes; resume only when the triggering Gen1 ×1 condition no longer remains and the relevant transport gates can be evaluated honestly. |
| H2 | Host-staged p99 cannot beat exact CPU fallback for any useful batch class. | Empty and real host-staged round-trip distributions, including p99, compared with the CPU cold path for declared useful batch classes under idle, NVMe, CPU-compute, and 5090-like contention. | Reconsider mandatory remote participation. Apply the transport conditional gate: batch more aggressively and restrict B70 use to workloads for which it is beneficial. | Demonstrate a useful batch class in which measured host-staged p99 beats CPU fallback without violating correctness or the bounded-p99 transport gate. |
| H3 | W4 format conversion cannot reproduce the canonical expert result. | Tensor comparison of converted gate/up/down weights, scales, SiLU multiply, accumulation, and expert output against the canonical CPU or CUDA oracle. | Stop integration of that converted format; correct the conversion or choose a representation that preserves the canonical computation. | The converted standalone expert reproduces the canonical result under agreed, recorded numerical acceptance criteria and passes the tensor gate. |
| H4 | Cross-vendor numerical drift changes route or token behavior materially. | Four-level correctness evidence: expert tensors, layer replay, long-sequence teacher forcing with routes/logits, and greedy generation/quality evaluation. | Stop accepting the affected cross-vendor, mixed-quantization, or reduced-return path; characterize the error and restore canonical behavior. | The drift is bounded and characterized, route/token behavior is no longer materially changed, and explicit quality approval is recorded where required. |
| H5 | B70 queueing serializes the 5090 critical path. | End-to-end timelines and p95/p99 layer/service measurements with one and four B70s, concurrent requests, foreground deadlines, and state-owner load. | Reconsider scheduling or remove mandatory B70 participation from the critical path; do not infer success from standalone kernel speed. | End-to-end evidence shows the queue no longer serializes the 5090 critical path and scheduling lowers tail time without correctness regression. |
| H6 | Normal warmed decode regularly reads experts from NVMe. | Warm-generation traces and expert-read telemetry across representative requests, memory pressure, and placement epochs. | Reconsider residency and cold-bank capacity. Do not describe the NVMe-backed path as robust interactive serving. | Warmed interactive generation performs zero NVMe expert reads in the acceptance run and the residency gate is sustainable for the declared workload. |
| H7 | B70 operation requires frequent weight migration instead of serving a resident expert bank. | Per-token and per-epoch transfer traces identifying weight movement, activation/result traffic, bank residency, and placement changes. | Reconsider the B70 role and placement policy; restore epoch-stable resident banks and keep weight movement off the foreground token path. | Representative execution shows B70s serving resident banks without frequent foreground weight migration. |
| H8 | More than a negligible number of requests require timeout recovery. | Request-level timeout and fallback rates, deadline distributions, quarantine/restart records, and outcomes under representative concurrent load. | Reconsider transport, worker reliability, deadlines, or mandatory remote participation; retain exact CPU fallback and explicit failure semantics while investigating. | Under a threshold agreed before testing, measured timeout recovery is negligible for the declared workload and late completions still fail closed. |
| H9 | PyTorch/XPU worker overhead dominates the kernel. | Worker-side profiles separating framework, submission, transfer, queue, and grouped-GEMM kernel time at relevant batch sizes. | **Preserve the architecture**, but replace the worker implementation with a native SYCL/C++ service around the vetted grouped-GEMM kernel. This is the explicit exception to architecture reconsideration when H9 is the only failed item. | The native service retains canonical numerical behavior and profiles show worker overhead no longer dominates the kernel. Other hard stops must still be evaluated independently. |

No hard stop is currently recorded as cleared by this document.

## Conditional decision gates

These are constrained responses, not declarations that the underlying risk is resolved.

| ID | Trigger | Evidence needed | Required response | Criterion to resume the broader mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 — B70 batch regime | The resident B70 expert kernel is slower than CPU at batch one but faster at batch $\ge 4$. | Standalone resident-expert measurements for batches 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, and 128, including median/p95/p99, numerical error, launch cost, bandwidth, utilization, power, and clocks, compared with the CPU cold path. | Use B70s aggressively for prefill and concurrent decode; retain CPU for isolated, latency-sensitive cold routes. | Mandatory batch-one B70 use may be reconsidered only after end-to-end evidence shows it benefits that workload without violating the correctness or tail-latency gates. |
| C2 — host-staged transport | Empty cross-vendor round-trip p99 is too high. | Round-trip p99 for 5090 D2H, host publication, B70 H2D, no-op/tiny kernel, B70 D2H, 5090 H2D, and completion; run with one and four B70s under idle, NVMe, CPU cold-compute, and 5090 attention-like load. | Batch more aggressively, use B70s as prefill/background workers, and avoid mandatory B70 participation in foreground batch-one decode. | Foreground mandatory participation can resume consideration only after p99 is bounded with no global synchronization and no per-request allocations, and useful end-to-end latency is demonstrated. |
| C3 — DDR5 capacity | DDR5 remains 64 GB. | An honest byte-level cold-bank model plus boot/runtime residency evidence showing whether the complete cold expert bank fits while required non-expert state and service memory remain available. | Treat GLM-5.2 as an experimental, locality-dependent workload; use a smaller model for production; do not describe the NVMe-backed path as robust interactive serving. | Broader GLM-5.2 serving claims resume only after memory is upgraded enough to hold the complete cold bank and normal interactive decode proves zero NVMe expert reads. |

## Feasibility assumptions assessed as strong upstream

The source design assesses these as strongly feasible. They remain evidence obligations here rather than resolved facts.

| ID | Assumption and risk trigger | Evidence needed | Required action if the trigger occurs | Criterion to resume dependent work |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | B70s can act as resident warm expert stores. Trigger: required banks do not remain resident and serving. | Bank-load, residency, eviction, and foreground weight-transfer traces under the intended workload. | Rework bank sizing/placement or reconsider the B70 role; do not normalize foreground migration. | Declared banks remain resident across representative runs and H7 is not triggered. |
| S2 | Each participating card needs only one small activation/result exchange. Trigger: the critical path requires larger, repeated, or weight-bearing transfers. | Byte-accurate per-layer traces by card, including activation, metadata, result, and any weight traffic. | Correct the protocol or reassess transport feasibility; keep weights off the token path. | Measured traffic matches the activation/result design and passes the transport gate. |
| S3 | CPU fallback is exact. Trigger: fallback changes selected work or its accepted numerical behavior. | Route identity, expert tensor, layer replay, timeout, and generation comparisons against the canonical oracle. | Stop using that fallback; restore the canonical weights/kernel/semantics or fail requests explicitly. | The fallback satisfies the exactly-once and numerical contract at all applicable validation levels. |
| S4 | Colibri can remain the reference implementation. Trigger: it cannot provide the canonical CPU or CUDA behavior needed for a gate. | Reproducible oracle outputs for tensor, layer, teacher-forcing, and generation comparisons. | Do not approve candidate paths without a stable oracle; repair or establish the canonical reference first. | The relevant oracle is reproducible and candidate comparisons can be rerun deterministically. |
| S5 | Substantial prefill and concurrent-throughput gains are feasible. Trigger: end-to-end workloads show no such gain or tails regress. | Production workload matrix measuring TTFT, decode, p95/p99, throughput, transfers, queueing, and output agreement. | Restrict B70 use to demonstrated beneficial modes or reconsider the architecture if a hard stop applies. | Representative end-to-end evidence demonstrates gains without correctness or tail regression. |
| S6 | Traffic-calibrated placement is feasible. Trigger: placement is based on assumed balance or worsens measured layer tails/migration. | Persistent route traces, per-layer frequencies, measured device cost, coactivation, migration, and replay results. | Revert to the last evidence-backed placement and recalibrate from measured traffic. | The same route-trace replay shows lower p95/p99 without correctness regression or migration instability. |
| S7 | Weight migration can be kept off the token path. Trigger: normal foreground tokens wait on expert weight movement. | Foreground timelines plus device/host/NVMe weight-transfer telemetry during warmed generation. | Restore resident/cold-bank coverage, narrow the workload, or reconsider the design. | Warmed execution serves selected experts without foreground weight migration or NVMe reads. |

## Plausible but unproven feasibility assumptions

These items are explicitly unproven. They must remain open until direct evidence satisfies the listed criterion.

| ID | Assumption and risk trigger | Evidence needed | Required action if the trigger occurs | Criterion to resume dependent claims |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Large batch-one decode gains are possible. Trigger: end-to-end batch-one performance does not improve. | Batch-one decode latency and tails with real routing, joining, queueing, transfers, CPU fallback comparison, and correctness checks. | Follow C1/C2: retain CPU for isolated latency-sensitive routes and use B70s for prefill/concurrency/background work. | Representative end-to-end batch-one measurements show a gain without violating correctness or tail gates. |
| P2 | The system may retain 80–90% of an ideal all-local path. Trigger: measured retention falls outside that target. | A defined ideal all-local baseline and like-for-like end-to-end throughput/latency measurements including all transport and join costs. | Do not repeat the target as achieved; identify critical-path loss and narrow performance claims or redesign. | Repeat measurements meet the stated 80–90% target without excluding required system costs. |
| P3 | CUDA-to-Level-Zero p99 may be sufficiently low. Trigger: C2 or H2 occurs. | The complete empty and real round-trip distributions under all specified contention modes. | Apply C2, and apply H2 if no useful batch class beats CPU fallback. | The bounded-p99 transport gate and a useful end-to-end batch class both pass. |
| P4 | W4 GLM expert execution on B70 XMX may be efficient. Trigger: conversion is incorrect or resident execution is not competitive at relevant batches. | Canonical tensor comparison plus batches 1–128 measuring latency distributions, effective weight bandwidth, XMX utilization, prepack/launch cost, power, and clocks. | Stop integration on correctness failure; otherwise apply C1 or restrict B70 use to the beneficial batch classes. | H3 is cleared by evidence and the standalone kernel beats the CPU cold path at declared relevant batch sizes. |
| P5 | Four-card simultaneous submission may be stable on this motherboard topology. Trigger: DMA, compute, links, or thermals become unstable under simultaneous five-GPU load. | Per-card topology, DMA, compute, contention, power, clock, and thermal evidence with all devices active. | Stop multi-card integration and repair topology/driver/resource contention or reduce participation honestly. | Every B70 sustains stable independent DMA and compute under the simultaneous-load gate. |
| P6 | Local reduced outputs may avoid meaningful generation divergence. Trigger: reduced-return output materially changes routes, logits, tokens, or quality. | All four correctness levels, long-sequence teacher forcing, greedy replay, and quality benchmarks against full canonical output. | Disable reduced-return mode; explicit quality approval is mandatory before retrying acceptance. | Drift is bounded and characterized, no material generation divergence remains, and explicit quality approval is recorded. |

## Invalid planning assumptions

These premises are prohibited under the current design. The “resume” criterion means the dependent analysis may continue after removing the invalid premise or obtaining the demanded proof; it does not mark the premise as currently true.

| ID | Invalid assumption / trigger | Evidence needed | Required action | Criterion to resume dependent work |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | Treating the devices as 160 GB of unified VRAM. | Per-device memory visibility, ownership, capacity, and transfer semantics. | Model separate device-local banks and explicit transport; do not sum capacities into a unified pool. | Recompute the plan with device-local capacities and explicit movement. |
| I2 | Assuming direct NVIDIA–Intel peer-to-peer transfer without proof. | A demonstrated supported direct path with correctness and latency evidence on this exact topology and software stack. | Use measured host staging until proof exists. | Either the direct path is proven end to end or all dependent claims are rewritten around host staging. |
| I3 | Using NVMe as an ordinary per-token expert tier. | Warm-generation expert-read traces and end-to-end stall evidence. | Confine NVMe to startup, recovery, and background staging; fix residency/cold-bank coverage. | Normal warmed decode records zero NVMe expert reads. |
| I4 | Assuming 64 GB DDR5 holds the full cold model. | Byte-level weights, metadata, non-expert state, buffers, and service-headroom accounting, confirmed at runtime. | Apply C3: make GLM-5.2 experimental/locality-dependent and use a smaller production model unless memory is upgraded. | The complete cold bank demonstrably fits after an adequate memory upgrade, or the supported model is reduced to one that fits. |
| I5 | Assuming top-8 routing distributes evenly by itself. | Layer- and expert-level route frequencies, coactivation, session traces, and measured queue/device cost. | Place and replicate from measured traffic; do not capacity-plan from uniformity. | The placement plan is regenerated and replayed from observed traffic distributions. |
| I6 | Assuming a faster SSD automatically improves decode. | End-to-end decode timelines separating NVMe, CPU, transport, queue, kernel, and state-owner costs. | Remove SSD speed from decode claims unless foreground evidence identifies it as the limiter; keep normal decode off NVMe. | Claims are based on end-to-end measurements and warmed decode still satisfies the zero-NVMe-read gate. |
| I7 | Treating four B70s as one bandwidth-coherent 2.4 TB/s device. | Per-card bandwidth, links, queues, transfer paths, and simultaneous-submission behavior. | Schedule separate workers and banks using measured per-device costs. | Capacity/performance analysis uses per-card measurements and explicit transport rather than aggregate marketing bandwidth. |
| I8 | Tensor-level splitting of gate/up/down across remote devices. | No evidence can make the assumption part of the current approved design; the architecture assigns whole routed expert work and joins weighted partials. | Keep gate/up/down for an expert on its assigned execution path; partition at selected-route/expert granularity. | Dependent work resumes after it uses whole-expert route assignment and the exactly-once join contract. |
| I9 | Using marketing peak TOPS to predict batch-one MoE latency. | Measured resident-expert and end-to-end batch-one latency, tails, bandwidth, launch, transport, and queueing. | Remove TOPS-derived latency claims and benchmark the actual workload. | Performance claims are rebuilt from the required measured distributions and correctness gates. |

## Risk handling rules

1. A correctness failure never becomes a performance tradeoff. Late, duplicate, mismatched, missing, or numerically unacceptable work follows [`correctness.md`](correctness.md) and fails closed.
2. A conditional response narrows the operating mode; it does not clear the corresponding risk.
3. No threshold is chosen after seeing results. Terms lacking numeric values must be defined in the measurement plan before execution.
4. Kernel speed alone cannot clear transport, queueing, residency, multi-turn, or service-level risks.
5. Evidence must cover the relevant contention and concurrency modes, not only an idle one-token case.
6. H9's native-worker response is valid only when H9 is the sole hard-stop failure. Any other hard stop still requires architecture reconsideration.
7. This register remains open until measurements are recorded elsewhere; the design source alone is not proof of resolution.
